Youth Soccer Warmup Ideas: 7 Warmups That Take 2 Minutes to Set Up

For coaches of 6–14 year olds  ·  6 min read  ·  Ball-based warmups, no laps

The warmup sets the tone for the whole session. Get it right and the kids are engaged, sweating, and touching the ball within the first two minutes. Get it wrong — running boring laps, standing in lines — and you spend the rest of practice trying to win their attention back.

This guide gives you seven youth soccer warmups that need almost no setup, work for ages 6 to 14, and put a ball at every player's feet from the start.

The golden rule of youth warmups: every player has a ball, and nobody waits in a line. If kids are standing still, the warmup is already failing.

Why Ball-Based Warmups Beat Laps

Running laps warms up the body but wastes your most valuable resource: ball contact time. Young players improve through touches, and a warmup is 8–10 free minutes of them. A good warmup raises the heart rate, gets joints moving, and gives every kid 100+ touches before the first drill even starts.

Keep warmups to 6–10 minutes. Long enough to break a sweat, short enough that energy stays high for the real work.

The 7 Warmups

1. Dribble Tag

5–6 minAll ages1 ball per player

Setup: A 20x20 yard grid. Every player dribbles inside it.

How to play: Pick 2–3 taggers without a ball. Tagged players freeze with their ball under one foot until a teammate dribbles around them to free them.

Why it works: Instant movement, head up, dribbling under light pressure — the perfect first two minutes.

2. Red Light, Green Light

4–5 minU6–U101 ball per player

Setup: Players line up across one end of a grid, each with a ball.

How to play: "Green light" — dribble forward. "Red light" — stop the ball dead under your foot. Anyone whose ball is still rolling on red goes back a few steps. Add "yellow light" for slow-motion dribbling to keep them laughing.

Why it works: Teaches stopping the ball and control while feeling like a playground game.

3. Knee Tag

3–4 minAll agesNo balls (movement prep)

Setup: Pairs, no balls, scattered in a grid.

How to play: Each player tries to gently tap their partner's knees while protecting their own. Great as a 2-minute dynamic burst before the ball comes out.

Why it works: Quick footwork, change of direction, and laughter — wakes the body up fast.

4. Around the World Footwork

4–5 minU8+1 ball per player

Setup: Players spread out with a ball at their feet.

How to play: Call out moves and have everyone do them on the spot — toe taps, inside-foot taps side to side, rolling the ball sole-to-sole, then "freeze." Build up speed as you go.

Why it works: Activates the feet and ankles and builds ball familiarity with zero setup.

5. Colour Cones

5–6 minU6–U12Cones in 3–4 colours, 1 ball each

Setup: Scatter coloured cones across a 25x25 grid. Players dribble freely.

How to play: Call a colour; players sprint-dribble to touch a cone of that colour. Call colours quickly to keep the pace high.

Why it works: Dribbling with head up, reacting, and changing direction at speed.

6. Pass and Move Triangles

5–6 minU10+1 ball per group of 3

Setup: Groups of three, one ball, in a small triangle of cones.

How to play: Pass, then immediately move to a new cone. The ball never stops; players are always passing into space and following their pass.

Why it works: Warms up passing, first touch, and movement off the ball for older groups.

7. Sharks and Minnows

5–7 minAll ages1 ball per minnow

Setup: A grid with two safe end zones. 1–2 "sharks" in the middle without a ball.

How to play: Minnows dribble from one safe zone to the other without the sharks kicking their ball out. Anyone who loses their ball becomes a shark. Last minnow standing wins.

Why it works: Dribbling under real pressure, shielding the ball, and a competitive finish to fire everyone up.

How to Build Your Warmup

You don't need all seven. A great youth warmup is just two or three of these stacked together:

  1. Movement burst (2–3 min): Knee Tag or Around the World to get blood flowing.
  2. Free dribbling game (3–4 min): Dribble Tag or Colour Cones for touches and head-up dribbling.
  3. Competitive finish (3–4 min): Sharks and Minnows to raise the intensity right before your first drill.

That's a complete 8–10 minute warmup that flows straight into the session.

Three Quick Coaching Tips

  • Set up before they arrive. Cones down and grid marked means you start the second they're laced up.
  • Demonstrate, don't lecture. Show the game in 15 seconds and start playing — they'll learn the rest as they go.
  • End on a high. Finish the warmup with the most competitive, exciting game so they roll into the drills wanting more.
