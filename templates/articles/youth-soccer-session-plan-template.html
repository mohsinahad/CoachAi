The 4-Part Session Structure

Almost every effective youth session follows the same shape, regardless of age. The proportions shift, but the four parts stay the same:

PartTime (60 min)Purpose
1. Warmup8–10 minRaise heart rate and get touches with a fun, ball-based game.
2. Skill drill15–20 minTeach or sharpen one specific skill in a focused activity.
3. Small-sided game20–25 minApply the skill in a real game with constraints that encourage it.
4. Cooldown & debrief5 minCalm down, stretch, and reinforce one takeaway.

A Worked Example: U10 Passing Session

Here's the template filled in for a 60-minute U10 session focused on passing.

Focus: Passing to a teammate's feet

  1. Warmup (8 min): Dribble Tag, then 4 minutes of pairs passing while moving.
  2. Skill drill (17 min): Pass-and-move triangles in groups of three. Coaching point: "Pass to the foot away from the defender."
  3. Small-sided game (25 min): 4v4 with a two-touch maximum. The constraint forces players to pass instead of dribbling into trouble. Rotate teams every 5 minutes.
  4. Cooldown (5 min): Light juggling and stretching. Ask: "When was a pass easy to receive today, and why?"

How to Adapt It by Age

  • U6–U8: Shorten everything. More warmup-style games, shorter drills (5–6 min each), and a longer small-sided game. Keep it playful — one coaching point per activity, max.
  • U10–U12: The proportions above work perfectly. Add a clear constraint to the game (two-touch, must pass before shooting) so the focus skill shows up naturally.
  • U14+: Longer skill phases, more tactical constraints, and a debrief that asks players to explain decisions rather than just recall them.

Why the Small-Sided Game Matters Most

If you're short on time, protect the small-sided game. Kids learn soccer by playing soccer. The drill teaches the skill in isolation; the game is where they discover when and why to use it. A clever constraint — like "two-touch only" or "you must pass before you can score" — turns a regular scrimmage into targeted practice without it feeling like a lesson.
