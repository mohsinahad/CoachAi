U8 Soccer Drills for Beginners: 6 Practices Any Parent Can Run

For coaches of 6–8 year olds  ·  7 min read  ·  No coaching experience needed

You agreed to coach the U8 team. Now you're staring at 12 six-year-olds with too much energy and a bag of balls, wondering what on earth to actually do for the next hour.

This guide gives you six drills that work for 6–8 year olds — simple enough to explain in 30 seconds, active enough to keep kids engaged, and focused enough to actually teach something.

At U8, every child should have a ball or be moving at all times. No lines. No waiting. If a kid is standing still for more than 10 seconds, change the drill.

What U8 Players Are Ready For

Kids aged 6–8 are learning how their bodies work. They can dribble, kick, and chase — but asking them to "play their position" or "look for space" is too abstract. Keep it physical and fun. The best U8 drills feel like games, not exercises.

Attention spans are short — 6 minutes per activity is plenty. Plan to switch things up every 5–7 minutes and you'll keep energy high for the full session.

The 6 Drills

1. Dribble Tag

5–7 minAll playersEach player needs a ball

Setup: 20x20 yard grid. Every player has a ball and dribbles freely inside the grid.

How to play: Pick 2–3 taggers (no ball). Taggers try to tag dribblers. If you're tagged, freeze in place. A free dribbler can unfreeze you by dribbling around you.

What it teaches: Keeping the ball close, looking up while dribbling, changing direction under pressure.

Coaching cue: "Keep the ball close — small touches!"

2. Traffic Cones Slalom

6–8 minSmall groups of 3–4Cones, 1 ball per player

Setup: Set up 6 cones in a line, 2 yards apart. Each player has their own line of cones.

How to play: Players dribble in and out of the cones to the end, then dribble back. Make it a race between groups — first team where all players finish wins a point.

What it teaches: Close ball control, using both feet, changing direction.

Coaching cue: "Use the outside of your foot on the way out, inside on the way back."

3. Colour Cones

5–6 minAll playersCones in 3–4 colours, 1 ball per player

Setup: Scatter coloured cones randomly across a 25x25 grid. Players dribble freely.

How to play: Call out a colour. Players must dribble to and touch a cone of that colour as fast as possible. Last player there does 5 toe taps on the ball. Keep the pace fast — call the next colour quickly.

What it teaches: Dribbling with head up, reacting quickly, changing direction at speed.

Coaching cue: "Look up so you can see where the cones are!"

4. Island Passing

7–8 minPairs1 ball per pair, cones for islands

Setup: Mark 6–8 small "islands" (2 cones each) spread across a 30x30 grid. Players work in pairs sharing a ball.

How to play: Pairs pass the ball between them as they move from island to island. They must stop the ball on an island before moving to the next one. No two pairs can share an island.

What it teaches: Passing accuracy, first touch, moving to receive.

Coaching cue: "Pass to your partner's feet — make it easy for them to receive."

5. Shark Attack (Small-Sided Game)

10–12 minAll playersPinnies, goals or cone goals

Setup: 3v3 or 4v4 on a small pitch (30x20 yards). Use small goals or two cones per goal.

How to play: Normal soccer rules, but score a bonus point any time your whole team is in the attacking half when a goal is scored. Rotate teams every 4 minutes so everyone plays.

What it teaches: Applying dribbling and passing in a real game, shooting, teamwork.

Coaching cue: "Shoot when you're close — don't be afraid to miss!"

6. Cool-Down Juggling Challenge

5 minAll players1 ball per player

Setup: Players spread out with their own ball.

How to play: Each player tries to keep the ball off the ground using feet, knees, and thighs. Count your best streak. Sit down and stretch while trying — it keeps the session calm at the end.

What it teaches: Ball feel, coordination, calming down after the game.

Coaching cue: "Soft touches — pretend the ball is made of glass."

Putting It Together: A Full U8 Session

A 60-minute session for U8 players works well in this order:

  1. Dribble Tag — 6 min (warmup, everyone moving immediately)
  2. Colour Cones — 6 min (fun, high energy, head up)
  3. Traffic Cones Slalom — 8 min (skill focus)
  4. Island Passing — 8 min (introduce passing)
  5. Shark Attack Game — 12 min (apply everything in a game)
  6. Juggling Challenge — 5 min (cool down)

That's 45 minutes of activity plus transition time between drills. You'll fill a full hour comfortably.

Three Rules for Coaching U8

  • Never explain for more than 30 seconds. Show them, then let them play. They learn by doing, not listening.
  • Give one coaching point per drill. Pick the most important cue and repeat it — don't try to fix everything at once.
  • Keep it positive. At this age, loving the game matters more than getting anything right. Celebrate effort, not just success.
